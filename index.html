Speaker 1 · Kiran Disrupting Normal Reality

The 1978 Delhi Tornado

March 17, 1978 · North Delhi Unprecedented

Amitav Ghosh witnesses a sudden, violent vortex tearing through the university campus.

Unprecedented Event

A sudden violent tornado struck Delhi in 1978 — a meteorological phenomenon virtually unseen in the historical memory of the city.

Shattered Reality

The extreme violence of the storm felt completely surreal and untellable to witnesses, defying their everyday framework of reality.

False Dismissal

Society quickly classified the disaster as an isolated "freak accident," avoiding the unsettling truth of an increasingly unstable climate.

Core Diagnosis

The climate crisis is not merely an environmental emergency — it is fundamentally a cultural, imaginative, and narrative failure.

Speaker 2 · Suriya Modern Fiction's Blind Spot

Literature & The "Unthinkable"

Why modern realistic fiction excludes climate extremes from serious art

Predictable Realism

The modern novel was born alongside bourgeois regularity. Stories prioritize ordinary domestic dilemmas, personal relationships, and predictable routines.

"To include a catastrophic storm in a modern novel is to risk being dismissed as melodramatic."

Improbable Extremes

Cyclones, mega-droughts, and sudden floods seem "too improbable" for realistic literary fiction, relegating the planet's biggest threats to sci-fi and fantasy.

"The irony: nature has become radically improbable, while our fiction remains stubbornly predictable."

Scale Blindness

Climate change operates across planetary scales and multi-decade epochs. Our narrative instruments are tuned only to individual human life spans.

"How can a 300-page story about two lovers comprehend a million-year carbon shift?"
Speaker 3 · Hari Bridging The Divide

Scientific Reality vs. Cultural Imagination

Closing the dangerous gap delaying collective planetary action

Current Divide

Siloed Knowledge

  • Confined to scientists and policymakers: Climate data lives in graphs, whitepapers, and COPs rather than everyday imagination.
  • Disasters viewed as isolated accidents: Each extreme event is treated as bad luck rather than systemic ecological shift.
  • Urgent public response repeatedly delayed: Information without emotion generates apathy instead of mobilization.
Status Quo: Paralyzed by lack of narrative resonance
Required Shift

Cultural Awakening

  • Embed climate into stories and art: Transform raw data into deeply felt human and community narratives.
  • Reimagine shared risk and responsibility: Move past hyper-individualism to recognize collective planetary fate.
  • Align everyday culture with climate truth: Make ecological sustainability the core intuition of our civilization.
The Goal: An awakened public conscience
Speaker 4 · Yogesh National Climate Strategy

Viksit Bharat 2047 & Climate Resilience

Core Imperative

National self-reliance and developed-nation status fail if our cities and agriculture are vulnerable to climate breakdown.

Economic Exposure

Unprecedented heatwaves, water stress, and erratic monsoons directly threaten GDP growth, workforce safety, and agricultural yields.

Resilient Systems

Construct climate-proof infrastructure: disaster-resilient transit, vertical farming, decentralized water storage, and clean power grids.

Proactive Readiness

Move from disaster relief to disaster anticipation — early warning networks, community-level drills, and bioclimatic architecture.

Evaluator Q&A Cheat Sheet

Q1: Why does Amitav Ghosh call it "The Great Derangement"?

Because future generations looking back will conclude that our era was deranged for recognizing climate collapse yet deliberately banishing it from art, literature, and politics.

Q2: How does the 1978 Delhi tornado prove his point?

Ghosh experienced it personally. It was violent and shocking, yet when he tried to write about it in fiction, it felt "unbelievable." Our minds are conditioned to accept only mild, predictable events in literature.

Q3: How does this link with Viksit Bharat 2047?

Economic development cannot happen in a vacuum. A developed India in 2047 will require sustainable cities, resilient food systems, and disaster mitigation, directly supporting SDGs 11 and 13.
